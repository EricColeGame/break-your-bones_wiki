## What the official page says the game is

The official Roblox record, retrieved October 14, 2026, lists High Effort Projects as the creator group, a place creation date of June 5, 2025, and a last update of October 13, 2026. The game supports up to 20 players per server and has roughly 133 million visits.

The developer's own description advertises a short, consistent set of features:

- Try to break all of your bones.
- A fun bone-smashing simulator.
- Unlock new ragdolls.
- Fling yourself from a huge drop.
- A ragdoll physics simulation.

That is the entire advertised scope. There is no mention of a code system, no currency names, no item prices, no damage values, and no shop. If a website tells you otherwise, that website is adding detail the developer has not published on the official page.

The core loop the description implies is straightforward: you control a ragdoll character, you throw it into hazards or off a very high drop, and the physics simulation responds. The bone-breaking framing is the scoring conceit — the point is to see how much of the skeleton you can wreck in one attempt.

## What "breaking bones" means in a physics sandbox

Physics sandboxes on Roblox are built around a simple idea: the character is simulated rather than animated. Instead of playing a fixed death animation, the ragdoll reacts to impacts with joints that bend, stretch, and collapse. That is why the phrase "ragdoll physics simulation" appears in the official description — it is the technical foundation of the whole experience.

In practice, this means outcomes are not perfectly repeatable. The same drop from the same height can produce different results depending on angle, speed, and how the physics engine resolves the collision. Players who enjoy this genre tend to enjoy exactly that unpredictability. If you prefer deterministic, skill-based platforming, this style of game can feel random by design.

Two advertised activities map directly onto that foundation:

- **Hazards.** The description says you try to break all your bones. Hazards are the tools for that.
- **The huge drop.** The description calls out flinging yourself from a huge drop, which is the headline attraction — a long fall into a physics collision.

The description does **not** document specific hazard types, a stage list, or a damage model. Anyone claiming exact numbers is working from their own testing, not from developer documentation.
