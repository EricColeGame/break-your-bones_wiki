## The Core Loop: Fall, Tumble, Break

The appeal of a ragdoll sandbox is that the character stops being a character and becomes an object. Instead of running and jumping with full control, you surrender control the moment you are launched, and the simulation decides the rest. Drops from height, collisions with hazards, and awkward landings all feed into that simulation.

The advertised goal — breaking all your bones — gives the chaos a direction. Rather than flinging yourself randomly, you are effectively trying to find the fall, angle, or hazard that produces the most complete destruction. That is where the replay value sits: the same drop rarely plays out identically twice, because the physics solver responds to your starting orientation, momentum, and where you first make contact.

Because the official page documents no damage numbers or bone-by-bone health system, you should verify how breakage is tracked by playing. A practical approach: pick one repeatable drop, record how many visible breaks you get, then vary a single factor — launch angle, run-up speed, or drop height — before changing anything else. That is a reasonable way to reason about a physics sim without inventing formulas the sources never gave us.

## Playing Well in a Physics Sandbox

Since the game is a simulation rather than a skill test, "getting better" mostly means understanding how the simulation behaves. A few habits help:

- Watch the very first contact point. Where your character's mass lands determines how the rest of the body follows, which is usually what produces the most dramatic results.
- Change one variable at a time. Height, angle, and speed interact, so isolating them makes your results meaningful.
- Use the 20-player servers to your advantage. Other players' launches are free information about what the hazards and drops actually do.
- Expect inconsistency. Ragdoll physics is deliberately unstable; a drop that shatters everything once may produce a mild tumble the next time.

Because the experience was last updated on October 13, 2026, the behavior you see today may not match older videos. Current in-game behavior beats any archived clip.
