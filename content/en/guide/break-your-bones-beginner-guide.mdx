The Roblox experience **Break your Bones** has a simple pitch: fling a ragdoll body into hard surfaces until the skeleton gives out. That premise has carried it to roughly 133 million visits since its June 2025 launch, and because the name sounds like several unrelated mobile games, plenty of people searching for it end up in the wrong place. This article covers only the Roblox version made by the group High Effort Projects, published at [Break your Bones](https://www.roblox.com/games/123821081589134/Break-your-Bones), and it sticks to what is verifiably advertised versus what the community reports versus what nobody has actually documented.

## What the Official Page Says You Get

The developer's own description is short, and it is worth quoting rather than paraphrasing, because most questions about this game are answered — or deliberately left unanswered — right there. The page advertises:

- Trying to break all of your bones
- A fun bone-smashing simulator
- Unlocking new ragdolls
- Flinging yourself from a huge drop
- A ragdoll physics simulation

That list is the entire official feature set. There is no code system mentioned, no currency name, no shop listing, no damage table, and no ragdoll roster. If a video or a wiki page claims otherwise, that claim is not backed by the developer's own material.

## Why the Naming Causes Confusion

Several products use near-identical words. There is a mobile title along the lines of *Break Bones: Fall Challenge*, there are Google Play bone-breaking apps, and there is an older, separate Roblox game called *Broken Bones IV*. None of these are this experience. They have different creators, different mechanics, and different progression systems. If you are looking for a game where you specifically fling a ragdoll off a huge drop and watch bones pop, you want the High Effort Projects place, and you should confirm the creator name on the game page before joining. Ripping mechanics from *Broken Bones IV* guides and applying them here will simply mislead you.

## How Play Actually Works, According to the Official Pitch

The loop the developer describes is straightforward. You take control of a ragdoll, send it into hazards and off a large drop, and the physics engine decides which bones survive. Broken bones are the score. Unlocking new ragdolls gives you different bodies to destroy, which is the progression hook the page advertises and nothing beyond it.

That is the whole documented design. Third-party guides describe additional details about map layouts, specific hazard placements, and which ragdolls feel sturdier, but those are observations from players, not developer documentation. Treat them as tips from strangers, not specifications. Community write-ups can be genuinely helpful for finding your footing, but they can also be out of date within a week on an actively updated physics game.

A sandbox like this typically reveals its own economy the moment you open the relevant menu, and that beats trusting a guide written about a version that no longer exists.
