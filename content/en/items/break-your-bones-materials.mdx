## How the Core Loop Works in Practice

Based on the advertised features and what players describe, a typical session looks something like this:

1. You spawn as a ragdoll-capable character.
2. You move yourself into a hazard, a launch, or a high drop.
3. The physics simulation takes over and the ragdoll flops, tumbles, and collides.
4. Bones break as a result of those collisions.
5. You earn progress toward unlocking new ragdolls.

The "huge drop" mentioned in the description is the clearest example of the design intent. It is a deliberate setup for the physics engine to do the work: you fall, you land badly, and the game registers the outcome. Players who enjoy this genre tend to enjoy it for the spectacle rather than for tight controls, because ragdoll physics is inherently unpredictable and a little chaotic. That unpredictability is the point.

The 20-player server cap is also worth noting. It is high enough that you will usually see other players mid-tumble, which adds a social element even without any advertised cooperative objective. Watching someone else's ragdoll bounce off a structure is, for many players, half the entertainment.
