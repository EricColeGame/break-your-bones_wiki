The Roblox experience **Break your Bones** is easy to misidentify. Several mobile games use nearly the same words, and another Roblox title uses a similar name. This article is about one specific place: the physics ragdoll sandbox published by the Roblox group High Effort Projects, which you can open directly at [Break your Bones](https://www.roblox.com/games/123821081589134/Break-your-Bones). According to the official Roblox record retrieved on October 14, 2026, the place was created on June 5, 2025, was last updated on October 13, 2026, supports up to 20 players per server, and has around 133 million visits.

Its premise is simple to state and deliberately silly to play. You throw a ragdoll character into hazards and long drops, watch the physics simulation take over, and try to break every bone in the body. That is the whole pitch. Everything below separates what the developer actually advertises from what players report and from what simply is not documented anywhere official.

## What the Official Description Says

The official Roblox description is short, and it is the authority on the game's identity. It advertises:

- Trying to break all of your bones
- A fun bone-smashing simulator
- Unlocking new ragdolls
- Flinging yourself from a huge drop
- A ragdoll physics simulation

That is the complete advertised feature set. Notably, the official description does not document a code system, currency names, item prices, damage values, or a shop. If you see a video or post claiming a working code list, treat it as unverified, because nothing on the official page supports it.

## How the Core Loop Works

The loop follows directly from the description. You spawn as a ragdoll, position yourself at a height or hazard, and let physics do the rest. Impact, tumbling, and collisions determine which bones break, and the goal is to break all of them.

Because this is a physics sandbox rather than a scripted obstacle course, outcomes vary. Two identical jumps can produce different results depending on angle, speed, and how the ragdoll lands. That variance is the point, and it is also why exact damage numbers are not something you can reliably quote. Nothing official publishes them.

The "huge drop" mentioned in the description is the most straightforward way to start. Height plus a hard landing tends to produce more breaks than a short fall, which matches basic intuition about a ragdoll simulation. Beyond that, the specific hazards and their effects are best discovered in-game, since the developer has not published a damage table.

## Playing With Physics, Not Against It

The most useful mindset for a physics sandbox is experimentation rather than optimization. Since no official damage values exist, there is no correct build order to follow. What you can do is vary your approach: change your entry angle, try different heights, and observe how the ragdoll responds.

A few practical habits help:

- **Aim for hard, flat landings** rather than glancing contact, since the goal is impact.
- **Use height deliberately.** The "huge drop" the description mentions exists for a reason.
- **Watch the ragdoll, not just the counter.** The simulation is the entertainment.
- **Replay the same setup** to see how much randomness affects the result.

None of this guarantees a full bone break on any given attempt. The simulation has variance, and that is by design.

## Is This the Right Game for You?

If you are looking for a structured progression game with documented upgrades and published stats, this is not that, at least not based on official information. If you want a low-pressure sandbox where the joke is watching a ragdoll catapult into the ground, the description delivers exactly what it promises.

One clarification worth repeating: this is not Broken Bones IV, and it is not any of the mobile bone-breaking apps on app stores. Those are separate products with separate mechanics. Mixing their features into a description of this game would be inaccurate.
