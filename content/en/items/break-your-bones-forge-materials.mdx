Forging is the backbone of progression in Break your Bones, and the forge materials you gather decide how fast your character actually grows. Most players hit a wall not because they are bad at combat, but because they feed the forge the wrong resources at the wrong time. This guide breaks down Break your Bones forge materials from the ground up — what each tier is for, where it comes from, and how to spend it without wasting a single unit.

Whether you are a few hours in or pushing toward endgame gear, the same principle applies: materials are a budget, and every trip to the forge should have a plan behind it. Below you will find farming routes, a priority order, and the mistakes that quietly drain your stockpile.

## What Counts as Forge Material in Break your Bones

Forge materials are not one single resource — they are a chain. Raw materials come out of the world, refined materials come out of the forge's first pass, and finished components come out of the final crafting step. If you only ever chase the rarest thing you can find, you will stall, because high-tier recipes still need a steady supply of low-tier filler.

Community reports consistently point to the same five families of forge materials. The exact names and drop locations shift between updates, but the roles stay stable, which makes this breakdown useful no matter which version you are playing.

| Material family | Typical source | Main use | Priority for new players |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw base materials | Common nodes, salvaged drops, starter zones | First smelts, practice crafts, bulk filler | High — never skip these |
| Refined bars and ingots | Forge smelting of raw materials | Weapons, tools, mid-tier armor | High |
| Alloy blends | Combining two or more refined materials | Durable gear, stat-focused pieces | Medium |
| Rare catalyst materials | Elite enemies, timed events, deep-zone nodes | Unlocking higher forge tiers | Medium-high |
| Boss-bound materials | Boss kills and group content | Signature weapons and endgame sets | Low until you can clear the content |

The takeaway is simple: raw and refined materials are the engine, catalysts are the gate, and boss materials are the payoff. Players who understand that ordering waste far less time than players who chase the shiny drop first.

### Why the "Boring" Tiers Still Matter

Every high-tier recipe in Break your Bones is built on a foundation of low-tier forge materials. A single endgame craft can eat dozens of smelted units that you could have gathered casually along the way. Stockpiling common materials early is the cheapest progression shortcut in the game — it costs nothing but a little inventory discipline.

## How the Forge Loop Actually Works

The forge is a three-stage loop, and knowing which stage you are in tells you exactly what to do next. Skipping a stage or rushing it is the most common reason players feel "stuck" with a full inventory but nothing craftable.

| Stage | What you do | What it costs you | Practical tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Gather and salvage | Farm nodes, break down unwanted gear, collect drops | Time and inventory space | Salvage duplicates instead of selling them |
| 2. Smelt and refine | Convert raw materials into usable bars | Fuel, time, and raw stock | Smelt in batches, not one at a time |
| 3. Alloy and craft | Combine refined materials into gear | Refined stock plus catalysts | Craft only what matches your current build |

### Stage 1: Gathering and Salvage

Gathering is where most of your real time goes. Salvaging unwanted drops is the fastest way to top up raw forge materials without a dedicated farming run, and it keeps your inventory from clogging with gear you will never equip. If an item is not part of your current build and is not a rare collectible, it is usually worth more in pieces than in storage.

### Stage 2: Smelting and Refining

Smelting converts volume into value. Because the forge works on batches, dumping a large pile of raw materials in at once is almost always more efficient than trickling them in as you find them. Player experience suggests that keeping a standing reserve of refined bars — rather than smelting only when a recipe demands it — removes most of the waiting between upgrades.

### Stage 3: Alloying and Crafting

This is the stage where decisions actually matter. Alloys and finished crafts consume refined materials permanently, so a badly chosen craft can set you back hours. Before you commit, check whether the piece fits your build, whether a cheaper alternative exists, and whether the recipe needs a catalyst you have not secured yet.

## Where to Farm Forge Materials Fast

Farming efficiently is about matching the route to the material family you need, not about running the hardest content available. A route that takes twice as long but drops three times the raw volume is usually the better trade.

| Route type | Best for | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter-zone sweep | Raw base materials | Low | Fast respawns, safe for low gear, ideal for bulk smelting |
| Salvage loop | Raw and refined materials | Low-medium | Kill, collect, break down, repeat — no node waiting |
| Elite circuit | Catalyst materials | Medium-high | Requires a stable build; community reports better odds in group play |
| Deep-zone nodes | Rare raw materials | High | Bring an escape plan; contested areas punish slow farming |
| Boss runs | Boss-bound materials | Very high | Group content; only worth it once your gear supports it |

A few habits separate efficient farmers from everyone else:

- **Farm one family at a time.** Mixed routes sound efficient but leave you with half-finished stacks.
- **Bank before you push.** Deposit refined materials before entering risky zones so a wipe does not cost you progress.
- **Track your respawns.** Nodes and elites follow timers; a route that lines up with them beats a random sweep.
- **Farm with a purpose.** Decide the recipe first, then farm exactly what it needs plus a small buffer.

## What to Farm First: A Priority Order

New players often ask which forge materials matter most. The honest answer is that it depends on your stage, not on rarity. This priority order keeps you moving without over-farming content you cannot use yet.

| Progression stage | Farm first | Save for later | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early game | Raw base materials, easy salvage | Catalysts, boss materials | You need volume, not rarity |
| Early-mid game | Refined bars and ingots | Alloy blends | Refined stock unlocks most starter upgrades |
| Mid game | Alloy blends, first catalysts | Boss-bound materials | Build-defining gear starts here |
| Late game | Catalysts, deep-zone rares | Nothing — spend it | Recipes are gated by catalysts, not volume |
| Endgame | Boss-bound materials | Excess raw stock | Signature gear is the only real upgrade left |

The pattern is deliberate: raw materials carry you early, refined materials carry you through the middle, and catalysts carry you late. Players who farm catalysts at hour two end up with a stockpile they cannot use and a gear gap they cannot close.

## Mistakes That Waste Forge Materials

Even careful players lose materials to a handful of predictable habits. Fixing these costs nothing and saves a surprising amount of farming time.

| Mistake | What it costs you | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Selling raw materials for quick currency | Slower forge progression later | Salvage and smelt instead of selling |
| Smelting one item at a time | Time and fuel inefficiency | Batch your smelts after each farm run |
| Crafting gear you will replace in an hour | Refined materials and catalysts | Craft only for your current build tier |
| Ignoring low-tier nodes | A permanent shortage of filler | Sweep starter zones between major runs |
| Hoarding catalysts forever | Wasted inventory and stalled upgrades | Spend catalysts when a real upgrade appears |
| Farming alone in group-tuned zones | Slower kills, more deaths | Team up for elite and boss content |

Community reports also flag a subtler problem: crafting the "best" item on a tier list without checking whether your build can actually use it. A perfectly rolled piece that does not fit your playstyle is still a waste of forge materials.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the best Break your Bones forge materials for beginners?**
Raw base materials and refined bars. They are easy to farm, they feed almost every early recipe, and they stay relevant later as filler. Chasing rare catalysts too early usually leaves you with materials you cannot use and gear you cannot upgrade.

**Should I sell forge materials or save them?**
Save them. Selling raw materials gives you a short-term currency bump, but it slows down every future craft. If you need currency, sell duplicate gear after salvaging what you actually need — not the materials themselves.

**How do I get rare forge materials faster?**
Farm elite circuits and deep-zone nodes with a stable build, and run boss content in a group. Player experience suggests that consistency beats luck here: repeated efficient runs on a known route will outperform random exploration every time.

**Do Break your Bones forge materials carry over between updates?**
That varies, and community reports are mixed. The safe approach is to keep your stockpile organized by family so that, if a recipe or tier changes, you can quickly see what still has value and what you can spend freely.

The bottom line is that forge materials in Break your Bones reward planning more than grinding. Learn the three-stage loop, farm the family you actually need, and spend on upgrades that fit your build — and the forge stops being a bottleneck and starts being the fastest path forward.
