## How a Session Actually Plays Out

Based on the advertised features and how physics sandboxes generally work, a session follows a simple loop.

You spawn as a ragdoll. A ragdoll in this context means the character goes limp and is driven by the physics engine instead of by scripted animation — joints flop, limbs trail, and impacts read as impacts. You then find a way to put that body into something dangerous: a long fall, a hard surface, a hazard. The goal stated on the page is to break all of your bones, which frames the whole thing as a completion challenge rather than an open creative mode.

The "huge drop" line is doing real work here. Height is the cheapest way to generate speed, and speed is what makes a ragdoll collision look dramatic. New players usually start by finding the tallest thing they can climb and stepping off. That is the intended entry point.

The unlock layer is the part that keeps people playing. The page advertises unlocking new ragdolls, which suggests you accumulate progress and trade it for different bodies to throw around. Beyond that sentence, the official record says nothing about how unlocks work, what they cost, or what any specific ragdoll looks like.

## Playing Well Without Wasting Time

Since the game is a physics sandbox, skill is mostly about setting up better collisions.

Angle matters more than height. A flat drop onto a hard floor and an angled drop into a corner produce very different results, because ragdoll joints bend and twist rather than absorbing everything head-on. Try to land on edges, corners, and transitions between surfaces.

Speed compounds. The page's own emphasis on a "huge drop" tells you the designers expect gravity to be your main tool. Chain a fall into a slide, or a fall into a bounce, and you get more impacts per attempt than a single straight drop.

Use the 20-player server cap to your advantage. Other players in a physics sandbox create chaos, and chaos creates collisions. If you are stuck on breaking something specific, watching what another player does is often faster than experimenting alone.

Finally, accept that physics is unpredictable. That unpredictability is the product, not a bug. If you want a deterministic bone-breaking checklist, this genre will frustrate you.
