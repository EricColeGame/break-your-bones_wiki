## Why the Physics Is the Point

The phrase "ragdoll physics simulation" carries most of the weight here. A ragdoll sandbox is not a scripted game where the same fall produces the same result every time. The appeal is emergent: joints, momentum, and collisions interact, and the outcome is the simulation's decision rather than a designer's animation.

That has practical consequences for how you play.

- **Expect variance.** The same jump may produce different results on different attempts. This is normal for physics-driven games and is part of the entertainment rather than a bug.
- **Experiment with angle and speed.** If a drop does not produce the outcome you want, changing your approach is more productive than repeating it identically.
- **Watch other players.** In a 20-player server, someone else's attempt is free information about what the simulation does.

What this article will not do is hand you a damage table or a bone-by-bone breakdown. The official page publishes no damage values or bone list, and inventing them would be worse than saying nothing. If such numbers exist, they are discoverable only by playing.
