## What Is Break Your Bones, and Why Does It Have a Group?

Break Your Bones is a physics-driven ragdoll game built on one beautifully simple idea: hurt your character as much as possible. Players hurl themselves off rooftops, step in front of traffic, and tumble down staircases while a counter tracks every bone they snap. There's no traditional win condition — the leaderboard is a scoreboard of suffering, and the real fun comes from everyone reacting to the carnage together.

Because the game is chaotic and social by design, its community lives in a group. Think of the group as the lobby that exists outside the match: it's where announcements land first, where events get organized, and where you find people who won't bail on you mid-ragdoll.

| What the group gives you | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Announcements | Updates and new content usually hit the group before anywhere else |
| Event invites | Community-run sessions, challenges, and competitions |
| A teammate pool | Fewer random lobbies, more people who actually play like you |
| Group identity | A visible tag or badge that shows you're part of the crew |
| Support channels | Somewhere to report bugs, scammers, or rule-breakers |

## Break Your Bones How to Join Group: Step by Step

Here's the core walkthrough. Exact labels shift slightly between devices and platform updates, but the flow is the same everywhere.

| Step | What to do | Where to find it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Open your profile or main menu | Avatar icon or side menu |
| 2 | Find the "Groups" or "Communities" section | Profile tab or menu list |
| 3 | Search the group name exactly | Search bar |
| 4 | Confirm it's the right group | Member count, description, verified badge |
| 5 | Click "Join" or "Request to Join" | Group header |
| 6 | Accept the rules prompt | Pop-up dialog |
| 7 | Refresh and confirm your membership | Your groups list |

**Steps 1–2: Find the community menu.** Open your profile and look for the groups or communities tab. On mobile, this is sometimes tucked under a "More" or "See all" link, so expand the menu before assuming it's missing.

**Steps 3–4: Search carefully.** Typing the game's name into the search bar often surfaces lookalike groups with near-identical titles. Before you click anything, compare three things: the member count, the description, and whether the group links back to the official game page. A verified badge is the strongest signal, though not every legitimate community has one.

**Steps 5–6: Join and accept the rules.** Some groups let you in instantly; others put you in a pending queue. If a rules prompt appears, read it. Agreeing to rules and then breaking them is the fastest possible route to a kick.

**Steps 7: Verify.** Refresh the page and check your groups list. If you don't see the group there, you're not actually a member yet — pending is not the same as joined.

## Desktop vs Mobile: Where the Join Button Lives

The Break Your Bones how to join group process is essentially identical across devices, but the menus are arranged differently.

| Platform | Path to the group | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop browser | Profile → Groups → Search | Best for reading long rules and pinned posts |
| Mobile app | Menu → Communities → Search | Some menus collapse under "More" |
| Tablet | Same as mobile | Bigger layout, same steps |
| Console / TV client | Limited group tools | Join on phone or PC, then play on console |

If you play on a console or a TV-based client, the group features are usually trimmed down or missing entirely. Join from a phone or computer instead — membership carries over to your account, so you won't have to repeat the process on every device.

## Group Etiquette: How to Stay In After You Join

Getting in is the easy part. Staying in is about behavior, and most removals come down to a short list of avoidable mistakes.

- Read the rules before your first post, not after your first warning.
- Don't spam join requests if you're denied — ask why instead.
- Keep chat on topic; most groups have a separate channel for off-topic talk.
- Never advertise a competing group in someone else's community.
- Report exploits and bugs to staff rather than using them.
- Be patient with moderators, who are almost always unpaid volunteers.

Community reports also suggest that many "unfair" kicks are actually the result of a private profile or a fresh account being flagged as a possible alt. If you're brand new, spend a little time playing before applying to a strict group.

## FAQ

**Is joining the Break Your Bones group free?**
In most cases, yes. The overwhelming majority of game communities cost nothing to join. A small number of groups operate paid membership tiers, so check the group description before you commit to anything. If a random player asks you to pay them directly for an invite, that's a scam — walk away.

**Can I be a member of more than one group?**
Usually yes, but every platform caps how many groups an account can belong to, and that cap can change over time. If you can't join, leave an inactive group you no longer use and try again.

**How do I know I found the official group?**
Look for a verified badge, a large and growing member count, and a link from the actual game page or its description. A group with a slightly misspelled name and a handful of members is almost certainly an imitation.

**How long does approval take, and what if I'm rejected?**
Open groups accept you instantly. Request-based groups can take anywhere from a few hours to several days depending on how active the staff are. If you're rejected, don't re-apply immediately — ask a moderator what went wrong, fix it, and try again later.
